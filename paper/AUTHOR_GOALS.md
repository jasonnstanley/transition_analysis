# Author Goals

## Purpose

This document defines the principles that guide the preparation of research
manuscripts accompanying the Transition Analysis Toolkit and related research
projects.

The objective is not merely to produce publishable papers, but to produce
research that is rigorous, reproducible, logically connected, and enjoyable to
read.

Research software produces trustworthy evidence.

The manuscript communicates that evidence through clear scholarly narrative.

---

# Core Principles

## 1. The Reader Comes First

Every decision should improve the reader's understanding.

The reader should never wonder:

- Why am I reading this?
- How does this relate to the previous section?
- Where is the argument going?

The manuscript should answer these questions naturally through its structure and
narrative.

---

## 2. Narrative Before Detail

Every major section should:

1. orient the reader,
2. explain its purpose,
3. present its contribution,
4. transition naturally to the next section.

Information should never appear without context.

---

## 3. Maintain the Narrative Thread

Narrative continuity should be maintained throughout the manuscript.

Each chapter should contain natural backward and forward references where they
assist the reader.

Cross-references are not simply navigation—they demonstrate the logical
development of the research.

The goal is for the manuscript to read as one continuous story rather than a
collection of independent chapters.

---

## 4. Chapter Roadmaps

Each chapter begins by explaining:

- what has already been established,
- what this chapter will achieve,
- why this chapter matters,
- how it connects to subsequent chapters.

Each chapter should conclude by preparing the reader for what follows.

---

## 5. Evidence Before Interpretation

Results present evidence.

Discussion explains meaning.

Conclusions synthesise contributions.

Automation generates verified facts.

Scholarly interpretation remains the responsibility of the author.

---

## 6. Reproducibility Supports Credibility

The toolkit exists to ensure that every published result can be reproduced.

Automation should remove repetitive work while preserving complete transparency.

The manuscript should focus on explaining the research rather than demonstrating
manual calculations.

---

## 7. Clarity Over Complexity

Complex ideas should be communicated using the simplest language that preserves
technical accuracy.

Writing should be precise without becoming unnecessarily complicated.

A paper should be easy to read without becoming simplistic.

---

## 8. Every Section Has One Purpose

Each chapter and section should answer a single question.

Introduction
: Why was this research undertaken?

Methodology
: How was the research conducted?

Results
: What was found?

Discussion
: What do the findings mean?

Conclusion
: What should the reader remember?

---

## 9. Continuous Improvement

Writing is iterative.

Each revision should improve:

- clarity,
- logical flow,
- narrative continuity,
- readability,
- scholarly precision.

The goal is not simply correctness.

The goal is a manuscript that guides the reader effortlessly from the research
problem to the research contribution.

---

# Origin

These principles evolved from the successful revision of the author's doctoral
thesis following examiner feedback.

Particular emphasis was placed on narrative continuity, chapter roadmaps,
purposeful cross-referencing, and maintaining a connected argument throughout
the manuscript.

The revised thesis was recognised for its readability, logical structure, and
coherent presentation.

These principles now form the foundation for future research publications.
